## Part 1. Code review

### 1.1 Prior labels (W0, W1, tau, threshold, binarisation)

**Benchmark reference.** `giles_replica.py:152-163` binarises `img > 0.5`, counts voxels in each half and calls a hit when the count exceeds `int(round(0.05 * vol))`. `deficit_slice` (`giles_replica.py:175-177`) sets y = 1 for W1 only, 0 for W0 only and 0.5 for both; the trial (`giles_replica.py:258-268`) makes a patient respond under arm `group` iff `group == y_true or y_true == 0.5`, so p1 − p0 is exactly 2y − 1 under TE = 1, RE = 0.

**Virtual-trial family.** `neuro_prior.py:139-155` uses `overlap > threshold` on the stored fraction, with `threshold` drawn in [0.03, 0.08]; both hit gives `both_hit_value = 0.5`, which `optimal_treatment` turns into "either arm works" (`neuro_prior.py:157-169`), so mu is equal under both arms and tau = 0. One hit gives tau = ±TE(1 − RE). Consistent with the benchmark up to the deliberate threshold range and the fraction-versus-rounded-count difference, which only matters for halves of a few hundred voxels.

**Cache.** `build_prior_cache.py:153` binarises with `raw > threshold` (default 0.5) and computes fractions with the same atlas masks; the cache records `channel`, `threshold` and `modality`. Checked, no defect, conditional on the cache having been built from the disconnectome folder at threshold 0.5. Confirm in one minute:

```bash
python -c "import numpy as np; z=np.load('pools/kch_s0/fold0.npz', allow_pickle=True); print(z['channel'], z['threshold'], z['images_dir'], z['overlaps'].dtype, z['overlaps_genetics'].dtype)"
```

**Theory family.** `intersynth_theory.py:276-294`: CATE = f·beta·D·(S_A − S_B), with S_A a Dirichlet-weighted mean over the 16 networks of genetics half-1 coverage and S_B the same for half-2 (`cohort.py:275-277`, `g4c_launch.sh:145`). Arm A is always the label-1 half, whereas the benchmark maps the larger half to treatment 1 network by network; since the weights span all 16 networks this is not a sign error, but the family never produces a tau = 0 class among susceptible patients (equality of two continuous scores has measure zero), its CATE never reaches ±1 (`effect_headroom` caps it at 1 − alpha ≤ 0.95, `intersynth_theory.py:245-247`), and its median |tau| is 0.02 (Table B.8a). Not a bug; a family that does not contain the scored process.

**Defect L1 (prior realism, confirmed).** The prior's class shares do not match the trial's and the launch script disables the check that would say so. Evidence:

- Zero-effect share among susceptible patients: virtual-trial family median 0.22 against 0.49 in the 32 real slices (`t2_partition_stats.csv`). Reason: random unions of atoms from different networks are rarely hit together, whereas the two halves of one real network are adjacent, so "both hit" is common. The leakage guard (`partitions.py:54-64`) is what removes the tau = 0 class.
- |tau| ≥ 0.95 share: 0.17 in the virtual-trial family (`tm_prior_gate_fold0.csv`, row `giles`; `p_te` 0.9 to 1 and `p_re` 0 to 0.05 put most effects below 0.95), 0 in the generic step kind (amplitude capped at 0.9, `generic_mix.py:175,183`), 0 in the theory family. The trial has 0.455 to 0.545.
- Generic step kind, the one family meant to supply the three-class shape: with `generic_noclip` unset (it is not in `g4c_launch.sh:145`), `generic_mix.py:163-165` clips p1 at the probability bounds. I ran `generic_process` with the launch ranges (400 processes, 50 latents): mean nominal amplitude 0.50, mean realised 0.37, half of the affected patients clipped below their nominal amplitude; the linear kind's share of |tau| ≥ 0.95 falls from 0.15 to 0.003 under clipping. So the "step" family is a smeared, continuous-amplitude family.
- `g4c_launch.sh:174-175` runs `prior_gate.py` with `--require share_zero=0:1 share_unit=0:1 ...`, which turns every default requirement (`prior_gate.py:38-39`: share_zero 0.35 to 0.65, share_unit 0.30 to 0.65) into a no-op, with the comment "G4c's prior is known to fail them".

Failure scenario: a posterior-mean estimator trained on effects that are mostly small and continuous shrinks ±1 effects and hedges tau = 0 upwards; the measured outputs show exactly this (Part 2, Q1). Confirm in minutes: run `prior_gate.py` under the final launch environment without the `--require` overrides and read `share_zero`, `share_unit` and the per-family rows; the run will fail the gate.

### 1.2 Latent scale (pool against benchmark latents)

Both paths call `ConvVAE3D.encode_mean` on the continuous map after `pad_or_crop` (`export_latents.py:79-83,98`; `ncpfold/encoders.py:173-176` with `binarize=False` for the disconnectome representation). Rows are aligned by basename in both (`build_prior_cache.py:172-176`, `ncpfold/folds.py:216-231`). No per-fold normalisation is applied when latents are written (`perfold.py:85`). `relatent_cache.py` and `build_fold_pool_cache.py` only swap `Z` rows by name. Axis order and sign: checked, no defect.

**Defect S1 (train/score normalisation mismatch, confirmed by reading, effect unknown).** Training standardises the whole KCH pool once with pool mean and SD (`cohort.py:253-255`, `standardize="pool"` is the default and `STANDARDIZE` is unset in the launch), then draws a process slice and rotates it. A training context therefore has a non-zero mean and per-dimension SD below 1 along the directions that define the slice. Scoring standardises each slice with its own statistics (`estimator.py:155-156`), so every scored context has mean 0 and SD 1 in every dimension. The model never sees a whitened context in training. The directions that carry the effect (those that distinguish the slice) are the ones whose scale is inflated at scoring. Failure scenario: a sharp learned boundary along a latent direction is placed at the wrong position when that direction is rescaled by 1/SD_slice. The fold-0 screen (Table B.9a: −0.015/−0.021 for the control, +0.029/+0.001 for the both-arms objective) is contradictory and single-seed, so the size is unknown. Confirm in minutes: draw 50 training contexts with `_build_prior(cfg_from_env())` and print the median over dimensions of |mean| and of SD of `Xc`; then print the same for ten scored slices after `PFNEstimator` standardisation (0 and 1 by construction). Fix at zero cost: `STANDARDIZE=context` in training, or standardise at scoring with the whole training side of the fold rather than the slice.

**Defect S2 (context duplication, confirmed by reading).** `context_replace=True` (default, `cohort.py:379-381`) draws a 3,000-row context with replacement from the slice minus the 64 queries. The pool has 1,207 lesions, the median slice 797 (Table B.8a) and `min_slice` is 72 (`cohort.py:236`), so a context of 3,000 rows is built from between 8 and about 1,100 distinct lesions, each repeated with independent treatment and outcome draws. Scored contexts never repeat a patient. Failure scenario: the model learns to average outcomes over identical rows (a denoising shortcut unavailable at scoring) and sees a far smaller number of distinct covariate points than at scoring. The only measurement (contexts 64 to 512 without replacement, Table B.9b) is confounded with context size. Confirm in minutes: count `len(np.unique(Xc, axis=0))` per training context in the same loop as above and report its 10th percentile.

### 1.3 Training target (head, loss, arm order, sign)

`cepo_ppd.py:21` builds 257 edges on [−0.05, 1.05]; `expect` (`cepo_ppd.py:43-46`) is the softmax-weighted mean of bin centres. A target at mu = 1 with sigma 0.02 is truncated at 2.5 sigma, which moves the target mean by under 0.001. `pfn_loss` (`train_pfn.py:78-86`) stacks queries as [arm 0, arm 1] with targets `[mu0, mu1]` and computes `cate = m[:, nq:] − m[:, :nq]` = mu1_hat − mu0_hat against mu1 − mu0. In every family mu1 is the T = 1 arm: `neuro_prior.py:237-238`, `intersynth_theory.py:352-353`, `generic_mix.py:261`. `predict_cate` (`inference.py:21-25`) evaluates `t1` for mu1 and returns `mu1 − mu0`; `PFNEstimator` returns `(p1, p0)` in that order (`estimator.py:195-197`) and the replica scores `p1 − p0` against `2y − 1` (`giles_replica.py:305`), where W = 1 is the arm that cures y = 1 patients (`giles_replica.py:258`). The context embedding carries T and Y as raw 0/1 (`model.py:53-54,63-64`) and the estimator feeds W and Y as float 0/1. Checked, no defect. The `sdpa` attention path (`model.py:22-38,68-76`) reproduces `nn.TransformerEncoderLayer` with `norm_first=True` exactly; it is not used by the final model.

### 1.4 Inference (context, encoding, cap)

The context is the whole training slice of the deficit and fold with its simulated W and Y (`giles_replica.py:663-681`); the cap is `cfg.pfn.context_max` = 3,000 (`estimator.py:123-126`) and the largest slice has 2,762 rows, so `cap_context` never fires. Queries are chunked by 512 with no attention between queries (`attention.py:17-18`), so chunking is exact. Outputs are clipped to [0, 1] (`estimator.py:197`), harmless for a head whose range is [−0.05, 1.05]. Rotation averaging and arm flip (`estimator.py:162-179`) swap `a, b` back after a flip. `CausalPFNEstimator` returns `(mu[n:], mu[:n])` = (arm 1, arm 0) (`estimator.py:337-343`). Checked, no defect. One note: `PFN_CONTEXT_SELECT=effect` exists but no result for it is in the snapshot; unknown whether it was ever scored.

### 1.5 Scorer (folds, clean extras, PEHE scale)

`group_folds` (`giles_replica.py:525-581`) tests rank-0 images only and, in `giles` mode, trains every later acquisition in every fold. `extras_folds` (`score.py:249-298`) hashes the validation images by patient to a pseudo-fold, uses stored fold k's training side as context and, in `clean` mode, removes every image of a queried patient; the lookup (`score.py:145-158`) serves `Ztr` of the one stored fold whose training side contains every requested image, which is unique because other folds lack the images they test. `score_predictions` (`giles_replica.py:297-318`) computes the headline as RMSE of p1 − p0 against 2y − 1 over all participants including y = 0.5, and balanced accuracy on the y ≠ 0.5 cases with the decision p1 > p0. Per-cell PEHE is averaged over folds then deficits (`giles_replica.py:744-747`), equal weight per trial whatever its size. The 29 Oct `fold_ids` fix is in place (`giles_replica.py:639-650`). Checked, no defect.

Two notes, not defects. The headline cell for classifiers is chosen on the released-code sigmoid scale (`giles_replica.py:722-726`) and reported on the paper scale; for in-context estimators there is one cell so nothing is chosen. The cross-fitted isotonic column (`giles_replica.py:397-419`) refits on fifths of slices that can have 30 patients, which is why it is worse than the raw PEHE for the final model (0.355 against 0.320 on receptor, `finalK_lin4p8M_effect_decomposition_validation.csv`); it is not a usable "ranking-only" bound at these slice sizes.

## Part 2. The "0.5 problem" (section 4 of the brief)

**Q1. Hedge or bias?** Both, and the measured part is small. From `finalK_lin4p8M_effect_decomposition_validation.csv` (three-seed means): the predicted effect averages +0.046 / +0.026 for tau = 0 patients, 0.67 / 0.78 for tau = +1 and −0.46 / −0.60 for tau = −1 (receptor / genetics). So there is a positive offset at zero and an asymmetric shrinkage, strongest for the minority class −1 (treatment 1 is the larger half, so +1 is the commoner class). Logistic regression shows the same pattern (+0.044, 0.70, −0.52), which says the asymmetry comes from the data and the latents, not from the prior alone. The best single rescaling recovers 0.010 / 0.010, so the bias that a scalar can fix is about a tenth of PEHE squared; the rest is within-class spread. Whether that spread is irreducible hedging or misplaced boundaries is measurable now with no new runs:

1. `effect_decomposition` already writes `mse_between` and `mse_within` per cell into `replica_results.csv` (`giles_replica.py:385`). Aggregate them for finalK and for TabPFN-S. If TabPFN's advantage is in `mse_within`, the PFN ranks patients worse; if in `mse_between`, it places the three class means worse.
2. Bin the test patients by distance to the nearest threshold (`coverage_diagnostic.py` already computes the coverage matrix and the bins at line 39) and tabulate the PFN's mean prediction per true class per bin. Shrinkage in the far bin (distance ≥ 2 thresholds, where a GBM oracle has MSE 0.011) is bias; shrinkage in the 0 to 0.25 bin (MSE 0.235 for the oracle) is hedge. The oracle itself puts 60 % of its squared error in the nearest bin on 28 % of the patients (`coverage_diag_effect_error_by_threshold_distance.csv`), so for the PFN the hedge share will be at least that.
3. Read the predicted outcome histograms (`logits0`, `logits1` from `predict_cate`) for near-threshold patients. A Bayes-optimal predictor under a deterministic trial puts mass at 0 and at 1 (bimodal) with the mean in between; a unimodal histogram centred near 0.5 means the model has imported continuous-outcome processes from the prior (75 % of its processes have continuous mu: the theory family, the generic linear kind and the clipped step kind). This is the diagnostic that decides whether prior realism (Part 4, P1) can pay.

**Q2. Class proportions and coverage near 5 %.** Mismatched, see Defect L1. In addition `verify_tau_by_family.py` and `prior_effect_magnitude_by_family.csv` measure |tau| but not the zero share by family; `prior_gate.py` does (`share_zero`), and `tm_prior_gate_fold0.csv` shows the only prior that met the trial's shares was the trial-matched one (zero 0.42, unit 0.36 overall; 0.49 / 0.43 for the trial kinds). The coverage distribution near 5 % in the pool differs from UCLH's for a second reason: KCH lesions are larger (median 10,409 against 7,122 voxels, Table B.8b), so more halves are hit and the pool's near-threshold band is thinner. Unknown by how much; `coverage_diagnostic.py` run on the KCH maps would give `share_near` per half for comparison with the UCLH values in `coverage_diag_coverage_probe_by_half.csv`.

**Q3. Output head.** The 256-bin head on mu is correctly specified for this target: for an ambiguous patient the NLL-optimal histogram is bimodal at {0, 1} and its mean equals E[tau], which is also the PEHE-optimal point prediction. A three-class head for tau or a two-class head for (W0, W1) with tau_hat = E[tau] would give the same expectation under the same posterior; it changes the parameterisation, not the target. Expected gain: none; cost: one hour per seed; I would not spend it. The CATE term (weight 0.5) is already the PEHE-matched loss; weight 2 was 0.005 / 0.011 better on fold 0 (Table B.9a), within screen noise, and untested at the final budget.

**Q4. Metric scope.** Consistent with the paper's Methods as described; see 1.5. The one thing an examiner may raise is that `pehe_paper` is the Methods convention re-derived, not the released code's scale; the docstring documents the choice and the anchors, so state it in the dissertation as a convention with its justification.

**Q5. Other 0.5s.** Disconnectome binarisation at 0.5 is applied identically in the cache and the benchmark (1.1); the encoder sees the continuous map, so the model must learn where 0.5 falls, and the band diagnostics show NMF on binarised maps resolves the band slightly better (wrong side 0.30 against 0.33, `coverage_diag_band_resolution_medians.csv`) while scoring worse overall. Family weights 0.5 : 0.5 and generic 0.5 interact with the problem only through L1 (they set how many zero-effect and unit-effect patients the model sees). The CATE weight 0.5 does not interact with the 0.5 class. The sigmoid in the released-code PEHE would, but it is not the headline. No hidden interaction found.

## Part 3. Design questions (section 5 of the brief)

**Genetics halves in the theory family.** Not leakage in the strict sense (no test patient, no label), but inconsistent with the project's own guardrail: the virtual-trial family was rebuilt on random partitions precisely so that the prior never trains on the benchmark's own halves (`partitions.py:3-12`), and the theory family then reads S_A and S_B from the genetics halves of all 16 networks (`g4c_launch.sh:104-109,145`). The receptor control is 0.004 / 0.006 worse on both splits with a seed SD of 0.002 to 0.004, so the family's dependence on the evaluated halves buys nothing measurable, and the family's effects are tiny anyway (median |tau| 0.02). Defence as it stands is weak; the cheap resolution is to show the model without the theory family (Part 4, P3). If that costs under 0.005, drop it and the question disappears.

**Pool source.** KCH is external and the separation of patients is sound. Two protocol points to report rather than fix: the pool is encoded with the fold-0 encoder, which was trained on the test images of folds 1 to 9 (outcome-free, anatomical only), so one checkpoint serving all folds reopens, weakly, the leakage B the per-fold encoders close; the measured difference against one checkpoint per fold is +0.001 / −0.004. The running ten-encoder pool does not remove this, since a PFN scored on fold k still trained on latents from encoders that saw fold k's test images. Say so in 5.3.

**Generic family at 50 %.** The dose-response has two points at the final budget (0 and 0.5 help, 1 hurts) and non-monotone single-seed points on the development track; the optimum is unknown. The family's own defect is L1 (clipping), which makes 50 % of a smeared family. Fix the family before tuning its weight.

**Why TabPFN wins.** Three mechanisms, in my order of likelihood: it was trained on tables whose targets are step and tree-like functions of a few features, which is the scored function class; its preprocessing (quantile transforms, feature permutations and an ensemble over them) makes it indifferent to the standardisation mismatch S1; and it reads 2,762 distinct rows per context, which it has seen, whereas our model has seen 3,000 rows drawn from at most 1,100 distinct points (S2). Initialising from TabPFN weights and fine-tuning on our prior over all folds is feasible (`finetune_tabpfn_prior.py`) and the risks are: forgetting (fine-tuning on the trial to convergence already worsened PEHE while improving the validation loss, Table 4.6); the licence (the script's docstring says the weights are non-commercial and the checkpoint stays under the same terms; whether a fine-tuned checkpoint may be distributed is unknown, check before archiving it with the dissertation); the choice of context size (1,500 to 2,000 rows fit a 32 GB card; the scored slices go to 2,762, and TabPFN's own preprocessing handles that at inference); and the contribution claim, since the reported model would then be TabPFN with a prior-specific adaptation.

## Part 5. What not to do

- Any input, token or auxiliary target that is the coverage of an atlas half, a half-hit indicator, a distance to the 5 % threshold, or a learned predictor of these fed to the estimator. The two-stage estimators in `two_stage_diagnostic.py` are diagnostics and must stay so.
- Training the prior on the benchmark's 32 partitions, or on partitions that reduce to them under a union (C2 is the check that this has not happened by accident).
- Keeping S_A / S_B on the genetics halves while claiming random partitions as the guardrail; either drop the family (P3) or draw its sources from random atoms too.
- Rescaling by a factor fitted on test or validation labels (`rescale_factor`, isotonic): bounds, not methods.
- Any UCLH test patient in a pool, including through a multi-encoder cache whose rows come from the UCLH training sides of other folds.
- Selecting P8's ensemble as the primary model after the fact; the pre-registered rule names the single model.

## Part 6. Ranked plan for one week of compute

Revised after the confirmations of 1.7: S1 is out, L1 (with the concrete repair in 1.7) is first, S2 second.

1. Day 1, no GPU. Aggregate `mse_between` / `mse_within` for finalK and TabPFN-S from the existing `replica_results.csv`, bin finalK's predictions by distance to threshold, and read the predicted histograms of near-threshold patients (Q1, diagnostics 1 to 3). Recount S2 on `Xc` alone. Score P10 on validation (CPU).
2. Days 1 to 2. Screens of P1 (the 1.7 repair, with the gate re-enabled), P3 and P6 (three 20-minute runs), each followed by a full validation scoring. Launch 3 seeds at the final budget for every screen that is not ≥ 0.005 worse than finalK; P1 and P3 are launched regardless, P1 because it is the only lever with a measured mechanism, P3 for the defence.
3. Days 3 to 4. Score the 3-seed runs on all ten pseudo-folds; combine P1 with P6 (distinct contexts, log-uniform length) in one run with 3 seeds, and P1 with P7 (CATE weight 2) if P7's screen holds. Run P13 in the gaps. P5 and P9 only if everything else is done.
4. Day 5. P8 on validation for the best model; `oracle_nmf_floor.py --tabpfn` on CPU in parallel to decide whether P11 is worth a later chapter rather than this week.
5. Days 6 to 7. One test scoring of the selected model (and its P8 secondary), paired tests, and the write-up of Part 1's defects as limitations where they were not fixed.

What is realistic. The measured levers that remain are each ≤ 0.015 and partly overlapping; two of them combined could bring validation from 0.304 to about 0.285 to 0.295, the level of logistic regression and of rotation-averaged finalK on test. Going below TabPFN-S (0.267) would need 0.04, which no measured or proposed lever supports in a week; 0.24 is the oracle for these latents and is out of reach for any method without the true effect. Say this plainly in chapter 5: the from-scratch anatomy prior reached the generic linear estimator, and the remaining distance to the pretrained tabular models is explained by the prior's effect-shape mismatch (L1) and the two training-to-scoring shifts (S1, S2), each now measurable.
